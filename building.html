FINANCIAL PRODUCT / BUILD STATUS

Real lending work.
Clear release gates.

Private build snapshot · 24 September 2026
Not a customer-facing market

The Federation is developing a bounded Solana lending program for supplying tokens and borrowing against collateral. The current prototype runs with synthetic tokens in a local validator. This is engineering progress, not a deployed lending service. The code and runtime reports are private, so the results below are self-reported and not yet independently reproducible by visitors.

LOCAL CHECKS PASSED

Supply and draw

Private local flows have moved synthetic tokens into market cash and out to a borrower, with recorded supplier shares, collateral and principal.

TWO LOCAL CYCLES PASSED

Interest and settlement

Two private local cycles collected borrower interest before paying suppliers and reserve, then cleared recorded liabilities. The second settlement used a supplier's reduced share balance after two withdrawals between periods. These are synthetic-token tests, not public lending activity.

BOUNDED FLOWS PASSED

Supplier withdrawals and exit

In one local test, a supplier withdrew all 1.2 million synthetic units of available cash while a loan remained open; repayment and settlement still completed. In another, a supplier withdrew 500,000 and then 250,000 units after payout, followed by a second full settlement. Separately, a market with one funded supplier completed payout and returned its remaining 1 million synthetic units at terminal exit. These are different test paths, not one continuous market history.

OPEN ENGINEERING

Later deposits and multi-supplier exit

Deposits at market creation have been exercised. Deposits after a paid period, withdrawals after the second payout, and a terminal exit with multiple funded suppliers have not passed a complete local flow. The prototype also has a fixed, operator-approved cohort of up to 16 borrowers and 16 suppliers.

OPEN ENGINEERING

Default and loss

Research records a reserve and proportional supplier-loss direction for this bounded prototype. It is not an integrated on-chain default or loss path. A non-paying borrower, reserve exhaustion, supplier claim changes and recoveries still need end-to-end proof.

DESIGN UNDER REVIEW

Liquidation and price data

The published liquidation specification has an unresolved trigger conflict. Oracle failure handling, borrower notification, emergency behavior, partial-fill limits and liquidator economics still need final rules and adversarial tests. No borrower-protective liquidation mechanism is live.

RELEASE GATE

Independent scrutiny and operations

Before any real-fund proposal: complete stressed and failure-case testing, examine reserve economics and operating coverage, obtain independent security and appropriate legal review, build a public interface, and record separate release authorization. No customer deposits or yields are available.

01 / BUILDERS

Review a mechanism

Examine a design assumption, model a failure scenario, or propose a test that could improve the specification.

Prepare a technical review

Use these headings to frame your contribution:

  1. Reference: specification version and section.
  2. Scenario: the conditions or assumptions you are testing.
  3. Finding: expected behavior, failure mode, and supporting calculation or evidence.
  4. Next step: a proposed change or test, including its trade-offs.

02 / BUSINESSES

Bring a real use case

Describe a financial workflow that is difficult today and what moving it on-chain would need to improve.

Start hereCurrent scope and development gates →Use the published stages to frame an exploratory conversation.

Prepare a use-case brief

A useful brief can be just four points:

  1. Workflow: who needs to do what, and where the process breaks down.
  2. Outcome: what a better result would look like and how you would measure it.
  3. Constraints: privacy, permissions, settlement, reporting, or integration needs.
  4. Question: which Federation project relates to the problem and what you want to explore.

03 / RESEARCHERS

Prepare an evidence review

Make the observation easy for someone else to check:

  1. Claim: quote the relevant passage and link to its source.
  2. Evidence: identify the data, date, method, and limitations.
  3. Result: explain what supports or contradicts the claim.
  4. Reproduction: provide the steps or public references needed to check it.
