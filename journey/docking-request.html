Mission 05 · Eno's path · About 3 minutes

The docking request.

A station offers your crew a free navigation map. Its terminal asks for access to your ship. How much access does a map need?

Eno · Exploration and independent thought

“Explore widely. Read carefully. Your curiosity does not require handing over your keys.”

A fictional permissions puzzle. No wallet connection, transaction, or real access request. You will never be asked to enter a password or recovery phrase.

Step 1 of 3 · Look at the request

“Activate your free map.”

Simulated access request

Display label: Free map activation

Requested permission: Let this terminal move up to 100 credits from your ship's vault, without another confirmation.

Your vault: 100 fictional credits

The label sounds harmless. The actual permission is broader.

Step 2 of 3 · Match access to the task

A map needs no access to your vault.

You check the station directory already aboard your ship. Its verified map service only needs your public ship name. You leave the original terminal and open that service.

Eno

“The question is not just ‘do I want this map?’ It is ‘what am I allowing this service to do?’”

Step 3 of 3 · See the consequence

From the story to a real wallet

Read what a request authorizes, not just its friendly label. Check the service, recipient, amount, and permissions. If you cannot understand a request, pause rather than approve it.

Never give a service or support agent your recovery phrase or private key. A recovery phrase can restore control of a wallet; it is not a login code to share.

This ship-vault model is an analogy. Wallets and blockchains handle permissions differently. Sharing a public address is different from signing a transaction or granting spending authority. Disconnecting a site may not revoke earlier on-chain permissions.

One final check

A “support officer” offers to fix your map if you send your secret recovery phrase. What do you do?

Mission complete · Understand the request. Protect the keys.
