QUESTION 13Is $GFOF "quantum-secured" or "quantum-proof"?

No, and the Federation will never claim it is. GFOF makes no quantum-security claims, displays no "quantum-secured" badge, and does not use "quantum finance" / QFS narrative framing. If GFOF ever publishes a quantum-security claim, that is by definition a corrections-log event.

What the Federation does instead is treat post-quantum cryptography as a research-tracking commitment, not a feature. GFOF's value is durable, auditable, on-chain records — and the long-run integrity of those records rests on cryptographic assumptions worth tracking honestly in the open. Solana transactions are authenticated by Ed25519 signatures today, which a sufficiently capable future quantum computer could eventually threaten; hash-based structures (transaction hashes, PDAs, Merkle roots) are far more durable. No cryptographically-relevant quantum computer exists, and credible timelines are genuinely contested among serious researchers.

The Federation commits to maintaining a public cryptographic-dependency inventory (which records depend on which assumptions), tracking NIST and Solana-ecosystem developments on the existing research cadence, and re-evaluating on defined triggers — not dates. No migration is planned or claimed. Anyone selling you a token on the basis that it is "quantum-proof" today is selling hype; the Federation will not be among them.
