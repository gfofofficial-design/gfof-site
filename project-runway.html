Method, assumptions and worked example

How the estimate works

  1. Deduct any one-time cost from available cash.
  2. Subtract monthly spending from monthly receipts to find net cash per month.
  3. When cash is falling, divide starting cash after costs by the monthly shortfall. The calculator rounds down to one decimal place.

The projection applies the same receipts and spending each month. Paydays, late payments, taxes and irregular costs affect the result only through the amounts you enter. In practice, a bill can be due before receipts arrive. A projected funding gap shows how much extra cash the plan would require by that month-end.

Worked example

A small studio has $24,000, receives $6,000 per month and spends $10,000 per month. At a $4,000 monthly shortfall, its cash covers 6 months. With a $4,000 upfront expense and monthly spending of $12,000, it has $20,000 at the start and a $6,000 monthly shortfall. That covers about 3.3 months.

The six-month spending limit is $10,000 per month for the current plan and $9,333.33 per month after the upfront cost. Divide the cash left after upfront costs by six, round down to the cent, and add monthly receipts.
