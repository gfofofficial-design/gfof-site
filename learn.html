Blockchain basics

A blockchain records transactions on a shared network. On Solana, programs run the instructions in a transaction and read or update accounts. An explorer lets you look up those public records.

Address
Identifies an account on the network. A public wallet address can be used for a balance lookup.
Token mint
Identifies a token. A name or symbol can appear on several mints.
Transaction signature
The lookup identifier for a signed transaction. It lets you check the status, accounts and fees in an explorer.
How can I tell two tokens apart?

Match the network and full token mint address to a source you trust. An explorer can confirm the account on the network. The project's official record helps establish which mint it says it uses.

Liquidity

Liquidity is how much you can buy or sell within an acceptable price and cost. A small trade may move the price sharply in a thin pool. Trading volume tells you what changed hands over time. Depth tells you what a trade of a stated size could get now.

Price impact
The price change caused by the size of your own trade.
Slippage
The difference between the quote and execution when conditions change.
Total cost
Check what you would spend and receive, the venue and network fees, and any amount needed to create an account on-chain.

Try a fictional trade

USDC is a token designed to track the U.S. dollar. In this fictional pool, a 100 USDC buy pays 10% more per token than the starting price when the pool has 1,000 USDC. With 5,000 USDC in the pool, the average price is 2% higher. This simple constant-product example excludes fees.

What should I compare in a real market?

Use the exact token and network. Compare both buy and sell quotes for your size. Check the route, fees, time, and minimum amount you could receive. Treat absent or stale quotes as an evidence gap.

Wallet safety

A wallet uses a private key to sign. Keep your recovery phrase and private key private. A public address lets someone inspect visible activity and may link that activity to you if you share it with your name.

Check before you approve

  • Open the service from a trusted link and verify its exact domain.
  • Check the network, asset, recipient, amount, and the permission the signature would give.
  • Pause if the request differs from what you intended. Use a public address for a read-only balance check.
A stranger sends a wallet recovery link. What can I do?

Close the message and open the wallet's official help page yourself. Keep your recovery phrase out of messages, support forms and chats.
