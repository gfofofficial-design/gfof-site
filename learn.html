Blockchain basics

A blockchain records transactions on a shared network. On Solana, programs run the instructions in a transaction and read or update accounts. An explorer lets you look up those public records.

Address
Identifies an account on the network. A public wallet address can be used for a balance lookup.
Token mint
Identifies a token. A name or symbol can appear on several mints.
Transaction signature
The lookup identifier for a signed transaction. It lets you check the status, accounts and fees in an explorer.
How can I tell two tokens apart?

Match the network and full token mint address to a source you trust. An explorer can confirm the account on the network. The project's official record helps establish which mint it says it uses.

Liquidity

Liquidity is how much you can buy or sell within an acceptable price and cost. A small trade may move the price sharply in a thin pool. Trading volume tells you what changed hands over time. Depth tells you what a trade of a stated size could get now.

Price impact
The price change caused by the size of your own trade.
Slippage
The difference between the quote and execution when conditions change.
Total cost
Check what you would spend and receive, the venue and network fees, and any amount needed to create an account on-chain.

Try a fictional trade

USDC is a token designed to track the U.S. dollar. In this fictional pool, a 100 USDC buy pays 10% more per token than the starting price when the pool has 1,000 USDC. With 5,000 USDC in the pool, the average price is 2% higher. This simple constant-product example excludes fees.

What should I compare in a real market?

Use the exact token and network. Compare both buy and sell quotes for your size. Check the route, fees, time, and minimum amount you could receive. Treat absent or stale quotes as an evidence gap.

Wallet safety

A wallet uses a private key to sign. Keep your recovery phrase and private key private. A public address lets someone inspect visible activity and may link that activity to you if you share it with your name.

Check before you approve

  • Open the service from a trusted link and verify its exact domain.
  • Check the network, asset, recipient, amount, and the permission the signature would give.
  • Pause if the request differs from what you intended. Use a public address for a read-only balance check.
A stranger sends a wallet recovery link. What can I do?

Close the message and open the wallet's official help page yourself. Keep your recovery phrase out of messages, support forms and chats.

Stablecoins: what supports the price?

A stablecoin targets a reference value, often one US dollar. The market price can move away from that target. Compare the backing, redemption route and who can change or restrict the token. Fiat reserves, crypto collateral and algorithmic designs have different failure modes.

A token trades at $0.97. Is buying it guaranteed profit?

No. A discount may reflect doubts about redemption, backing or market access. Fees, delays and eligibility can prevent a holder from redeeming at the stated target.

For a project treasury: record the exact mint and chain, issuer, redemption conditions, reserve evidence date and an operational plan if transfers or redemptions stop.

DeFi lending: collateral can change faster than debt

Some lending protocols allow borrowing against deposited collateral. If collateral loses value or debt grows, a position may become eligible for liquidation. Interest, oracle prices and protocol parameters affect the result. A displayed borrowing limit is not a personal safety target.

Try a fictional collateral shock

Assume $2,000 collateral, $1,000 fixed debt and an 80% liquidation threshold. The illustrative health factor is collateral value × threshold ÷ debt. At a 40% collateral decline it is 0.96. This simplified Aave-style model excludes interest, fees, multiple assets and changing parameters; it does not represent a Federation lending service.

Does a factor above 1 guarantee that a position is safe?

No. Prices and debt can change, and the oracle or protocol can fail. In this simplified model, a result below 1 meets the liquidation-risk condition; 1 is the boundary, not a recommended buffer. Check the actual protocol's rules.

Bridges: one symbol can hide different assets

A bridge moves assets or information between networks using a particular verification and custody design. The destination may receive a wrapped representation rather than the original asset. Check the source and destination networks, both token addresses, who controls the bridge and how to exit.

A bridge reports completion. Is the received token automatically usable?

No. Confirm the exact destination asset, wallet and network. Check whether the intended application supports it and whether destination gas and liquidity are available. Bridge security, delays and token redemption add separate risks.

Token controls: read what the issuer can do

Solana token extensions can add transfer fees, transfer hooks or permanent delegation. These features change token behavior; their presence alone does not prove fraud or safety. Check the token program, enabled extensions, authorities and whether your wallet and intended application support them.

If mint authority is removed, have all token risks disappeared?

No. Other controls, extensions, concentration, liquidity and application permissions still matter. A missing field in a report means unknown, not disabled.

For project teams: publish the purpose of each control, its current authority, who can change it and the evidence date. Explain limitations in ordinary language.
